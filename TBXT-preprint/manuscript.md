# Distinct cyclophilin A orientations around a modeled brachyury probe permit different fits of a single T-box duplex

**Ozge A. Cavus¹ and Aysegul Kuskucu²**

¹ AIstanbul Research Group, Istanbul, Turkey

² Department of Medical Genetics, Faculty of Medicine, Yeditepe University, Istanbul, Türkiye

Correspondence to Ozge A. Cavus (ozge.cavus@aistanbulresearch.com).

## Abstract

Brachyury (TBXT) recognizes a T-box DNA element and is a dependency in chordoma. We modeled two intact bifunctional probes extending from crystallographic TBXT pockets B and C to ask whether cyclophilin A (CypA) can fit alongside a reference DNA duplex containing one T-box element. For the same B molecule, restoring the resolved DNA-binding helix left 110 sterically admissible placements, including 78 that accommodated the chosen duplex and 15 in which CypA overlapped its interior. Under stricter filters, 15 of 22 placements still accommodated the duplex. The single interior-overlap example contacted both strands at base pair 11 of 12. For C, restoring the helix reduced interior overlaps from 26/481 to 1/121 under the primary filters. The surviving example contacted base pair 2. None of 56 stricter placements met the overlap criterion. We also tested identical near-native duplex placements in 200 frames from eleven short B-probe trajectories, with and without CypA in each frame. In the primary panel, 14/60 near-start frames accommodated DNA with both heads seated, whereas 10/60 original covering-start frames admitted a placement only after CypA removal. Each finding arose in one replica. A fourfold denser panel preserved that distinction and found both outcomes in the alternative covering start. In 166/200 frames even the denser panel found no fit after CypA removal. The same two head poses permit different steric outcomes for the reference duplex. Direct occlusion of bases responsible for sequence recognition remains unestablished. Solution populations, ternary binding and CypA-dependent inhibition remain open experimental questions.

## Introduction

Brachyury, encoded by *TBXT*, is a transcription factor on which chordoma cells depend [11]. Its T-box domain recognizes a short DNA sequence. Structures of human TBXT bound to DNA and to small fragments have made it possible to ask which ligandable surfaces could influence that interaction. Newman and colleagues identified pockets B and C within approximately 8 Å of the DNA interface and proposed growing fragments toward DNA [1]. Protein binders directed at TBXT can impair DNA binding, and oligonucleotide-based recruitment of VHL can drive TBXT degradation in chordoma cells [2,3]. A conference report also describes TAL61, a covalent small molecule that displaces TBXT from chromatin [4]. These findings motivate a separate structural question. Could a bifunctional molecule position an endogenous protein over a single DNA recognition site while leaving TBXT intact?

The RMC-GI-102 study of daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel as first-line treatment for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma prompted us to consider CypA recruitment in chordoma [13]. Daraxonrasib brings CypA into a complex with active RAS that restricts access by RAS effectors [5]. Here we asked whether a recruited CypA molecule could similarly restrict access by DNA to TBXT. A closer chemical precedent is a bifunctional RAS ligand that recruited CypA and blocked full-length BRAF while retaining binding to its isolated RAS-binding domain [12]. Recruitment of FKBP12 to an estrogen receptor ligand likewise permitted receptor dimerization and coactivator association [6]. For TBXT itself, an optimized A′-pocket series retained DNA binding under the reported assay conditions [1]. These results motivate a geometric question about two specific molecules. Once both ends of a tether occupy their intended pockets, can the recruited protein and a single-site DNA duplex fit in the same assembly?

We distinguish CypA overlap with a particular set of interior DNA atoms from simultaneous accommodation of the complete duplex by TBXT, probe and CypA. The former locates the recruited protein. The latter tests whether one full structure meets specified geometric criteria. Our calculations use a single TBXT DNA-binding domain and the single-element duplex of PDB 8CDN.

## Results

### Two placements of the same pocket-B probe give different outcomes for a single DNA duplex

The B probe links the C4 atom of the crystallographic 5QS1 fragment to a CypA-binding head derived from PDB 9GHY [1,7]. For each conformer we placed the two heads in their partner pockets before testing the protein arrangement against DNA. In Figure 1A, CypA overlaps a penultimate base pair of the 12-base-pair duplex. In Figure 1B, the entire duplex fits alongside both proteins and the ligand.

We first placed the experimental duplex at the same unshifted position in each TBXT reference frame and counted interior atoms within 2.5 Å of CypA. The original covering branches met that footprint rule in all 60 post-start frames. The near-start branches met it in none of 60, although both probe ends lay within 2 Å of their own binary crystal poses in 50 frames (Table 1). These are serial observations from different chosen starts.

In the near-start series, whole-duplex distances to CypA ranged from 5.67 to 7.96 Å. The same duplex penetrated TBXT itself in every frame (minimum distances 0.28–1.92 Å). In replica 2 at 0.5 ps the duplex lay 7.65 Å from CypA and 11.06 Å from the probe, while the TBXT Phe213 backbone oxygen lay just 0.56 Å from the O4′ atom of DNA residue C2. Clearing CypA in this overlay is therefore insufficient evidence that the complex accommodates DNA.

### Restoring the TBXT DNA-binding helix strongly reduces covering placements for pocket C

The two crystallographic fragment receptors ended at residue 211. The omitted C-terminal helix inserts into the DNA minor groove in the longer DNA-bound construct [1]. From 65,536 torsional conformers per probe we obtained 110 B and 481 C admissible static assemblies on the short templates. On these templates 8,614 B and 15,069 C conformers separately fit TBXT, and 16,917 and 17,656 fit CypA, at 2.6 Å protein–ligand clearance. The full comparison in Table 2 adds the 74 resolved heavy atoms of residues 212–221 from wild-type 8CDN to the same coordinate frames.

The added helix cleared the chosen displaced DNA by 3.118 Å for B and 2.660 Å for C. This is a superposed exclusion volume. The C211–N212 backbone junction measured 1.57 Å for B and 2.31 Å for C, so the transferred coordinates do not constitute a repaired covalent model. The TBXT core itself approached the fixed duplex to 2.752 Å for B and 2.691 Å for C. The static stricter rows therefore retain a prequalified 2.6 Å receptor–DNA register. A 3.0 Å gate on the entire receptor would reject both chosen duplex registers in every draw.

### Linker variation and conditional DNA scoring

Changing the B linker altered the number of compatible placements. In an additional 432,000 torsion proposals per design, 19 of 201 admissible reference-linker placements met the interior-duplex overlap criterion, compared with 109/814 for the longer linker, 69/551 for the E-alkene and 0/1 for the shorter linker. In the archived 50 ps trajectory panel, the two covering starts for each chemistry gave 42 and 6 covered observations for the reference linker, 51 and 51 for the longer linker, and 50 and 48 for the E-alkene, each out of 51 observations including time zero. None of the eleven available near/far-start trajectories acquired an interior CypA footprint hit. Seventeen of the eighteen planned main trajectories had been archived at the analysis cutoff. The missing reference-linker near-start replicate and the distinct starting geometries limit comparisons across chemistries. The reference-linker covering parents here are also the parents of the two alternative branches in Table 1.

We next allowed an 8CDN duplex to translate and rotate near its native binding pose while holding the modeled protein assembly fixed. Relative to the matched probe-only condition, the selected covering B arrangement retained 0.207% of the near-native DNA statistical weight under a steric model and 0.303% when screened charge interactions were included. Across four numerical sampling blocks the ranges were 0.108–0.278% and 0.210–0.396%, respectively. In the broad search, deleting the probe while keeping CypA fixed reproduced the exclusion, whereas retaining the probe alone preserved the apo steric weight. The selected C arrangement facing adjacent DNA retained the full single-site steric weight. Effective sample sizes in the four focused ternary blocks ranged from 3.8 to 15.6 for the steric integral and from 3.3 to 13.8 with screened charge. These conditional scores describe a single specified CypA placement. Converting the B ratio into a change in DNA affinity would require, among other quantities, the population of TBXT molecules that occupy that position.

### Calibration of independent ternary searches and local scores

Local AutoDock Vina optimization of five prefiltered full-probe starts per probe–protein pair retained the cognate head within 1.06 Å in 20/20 runs [8]. An independently embedded B starting conformer moved more than 7 Å from its TBXT head pose, and one independently embedded C conformer yielded no local CypA output. The crystallographic CypA ligand 19c and its N-acetyl analogue 19f provide an affinity control. The reported inhibition constants favor 19f approximately twelvefold [7]. Fixed-coordinate and locally optimized Vina scores gave opposite orderings. The local runs support compatibility of selected starting poses, while the affinity control gives insufficient calibration for assigning binding constants to the intact probes.

We asked whether the proposed ternary placement could be recovered without supplying it (Table 4). Across the original and longer B linkers, none of 96 active searches starting from separated proteins and probe seated both heads. Starting with one head seated, 192 searches produced one clash-filtered dual placement with the long linker. It had no CypA interior-footprint hit. These encounter calculations used simplified sampling and scoring. A binary CypA control itself recovered the reference pose in only 2/64 searches under the initial budget. A zero count in the ternary search consequently provides weak evidence about physical association.

## Methods

### Molecular models

We used human TBXT fragments NZ1 (pocket B, PDB 5QS1, wild type) and O0V (pocket C, 5QSE, G177D) and the 44-heavy-atom CypA head from human CypA bound to 19c (9GHY). The probes contain 64 and 65 heavy atoms, respectively. Each links its TBXT exit atom to the CypA head through the same neutral amide-containing connector. The covalent structures, atom indices and assigned stereochemistry are archived as SDF files and atom maps. In the binary-based C model Asp177 was replaced with the wild-type Gly177. The dynamics used one wild-type 6F58 TBXT domain, with the local 112–119 loop modeled from 5QSE and the 5QSE Arg54 conformation in the C model. The static B and C templates end at residue 211. We fitted the common TBXT core in 8CDN onto each static template and added the resolved 212–221 heavy atoms as a rigid exclusion volume. Each originally admissible draw was retested for helix–probe and helix–CypA clearance. This sensitivity represents one observed helix conformation without backbone relaxation. The trajectory-based analysis uses the longer domain and includes the helix already present in each frame.

### Geometry and definitions

For each self-compatible ligand conformer, we placed the experimental head groups in their cognate pockets and retained dual assemblies with minimum protein–ligand distance ≥2.2 Å and minimum protein–protein distance ≥2.6 Å. Direct transfer of the 8CDN duplex into the two fragment-bound TBXT frames produced clashes with apo TBXT. Before querying any ligand conformer, we selected the closest rigid duplex displacement that met a 2.6 Å clearance and retained native DNA contacts. The DNA shifted 1.50 Å for B and 2.35 Å for C, preserving 22/23 reference contacts in both. Alternate admissible duplex placements were also scored. For dynamics we aligned the crystal duplex and each frame on the source TBXT Cα core (residues 45–208, excluding 110–121) and corrected periodic images for the probe and CypA. A footprint hit requires that at least three different interior DNA heavy atoms spanning at least two distinct nucleotides lie within 2.5 Å of CypA. We excluded the terminal base pair at each end, leaving 410 heavy atoms in positions 2–11. We numbered the 12 base pairs from the start of chain C. For author residue number *n*, the paired position is *n* + 2 on chain C and 17 − *n* on chain D. Thus “two distinct nucleotides” can mean the two partners of one base pair. The post hoc sensitivities retained the same 2.5 Å cutoff and three-atom minimum, and either required two distinct paired positions in 2–11 or tested only positions 3–10 with the original two-nucleotide rule. For crystal-head RMSD we fitted each protein independently and allowed attachment-preserving head symmetries. A separate contact count used a 4.5 Å residue-contact distance relative to the source structure. For static DNA compatibility we required full-duplex clearance from TBXT (fixed at ≥2.6 Å), and from probe and CypA under the applicable assembly cutoffs. A failing interior-hit criterion alone does not meet the full-duplex criterion.

### Dynamics and sampling

Proteins used ff14SB, the full probes used OpenFF Sage 2.2.1 with MMFF94 partial charges, and solvent was TIP3P with 0.15 M NaCl. OpenMM dynamics used 300 K, 1 bar and a 2 fs timestep. The parent protocol comprised minimization, 4 ps of restrained equilibration and 50 ps of unrestrained production. The near and far starts were deliberately chosen to keep CypA outside the interior footprint. Each new branch reused its parent's terminal coordinates, solvent and cell, with new velocity, Langevin and barostat seeds and no positional restraints. Eleven branches yielded 100 ps aggregate propagation. We scored every 0.5 ps and excluded the copied origin from new frame fractions. Parent fractions for the linker panel include the initial observation. Replicas from one prepared geometry share its positional history. We report replica counts, cutoffs and observed crossings. The serial frames provide no independent binomial trials for equilibrium occupancy.

### Paired whole-duplex test in the B trajectories

For each saved frame we registered the component coordinates into the native 8CDN frame, corrected periodic images and held the snapshot fixed. We applied 8,193 candidate transforms to the same 486-heavy-atom duplex, comprising the identity and 8,192 scrambled six-dimensional Sobol proposals (seed 6280). Each translation component spanned ±2.5 Å and each rotation-vector component spanned ±8° about the DNA centroid. A candidate had to retain interior C1′ RMSD ≤3 Å and recover ≥70% of the 23 core Cα–interior C1′ pairs within 10 Å in 8CDN (each recovered pair within its own native distance +2 Å). Primary whole-duplex clearances were ≥2.6 Å to the actual TBXT, ≥2.2 Å to the complete probe, and ≥2.6 Å to CypA. The matched baseline omitted only the last gate. We also applied 3.0/2.6/3.0 Å and compared two separate halves of the proposal panel. Presence of at least one witness is an existence statement within this finite rigid search. Pose counts depend on sampling and are not thermodynamic weights.

### Fixed-assembly DNA scoring

The DNA competition calculation held one protein–probe assembly fixed and sampled a rigid, mobile duplex in the TBXT frame. The near-native end point required interior sugar RMSD ≤3 Å and ≥70% of the native contacts. We integrated the score-weighted poses in that region, dividing by the integral for the same fixed probe with CypA removed. A 12,288-pose common search was supplemented for the narrow covering-B region by four independently scrambled blocks of 32,768 proposals each. Models included native-contact and steric terms, with a secondary screened-charge calculation at 150 mM and neutral probes. The block spread describes integration sensitivity. Electrostatic approximations, limited effective sample size, the rigid duplex, and the unknown frequency of the prescribed orientation limit physical interpretation.

### Independent prediction and scoring

DeepTernary used published PROTAC weights, binary protein–head inputs in independent coordinate frames, and newly generated conformers of the intact probe [9]. DNA and the earlier ternary placement were withheld. The input adapter used chemical atom indices to identify each head. An initial series contained B stereocenter inversions and was superseded by a stereochemistry-checked rerun. The results here use that rerun. The prespecified joint criteria included ≤3 Å partner-aligned RMSD for each head, the internal-footprint rule, the steric clearances above, retained stereochemistry and bond-length tolerances. The independent 5T35 rescoring compared fixed protein orientations using each model's own complete MZ1 ligand. The native protein arrangement was used for evaluation. Differences among its scoring ranks describe performance on one control complex.

## Discussion

For a chemically defined pocket-B tether, the same two local head poses allow CypA to intersect the end region of a fixed reference duplex in one model and to accommodate the entire duplex in another. With the DNA-bound helix included, the sole strict B interior hit falls on base pair 11. The geometry leaves open whether the probe alters recognition of the T-box sequence. The same helix removes every strict C covering example in this sample. This makes the relationship between construct and outcome part of the structural result. A moving helix or a different receptor conformation could change it.

The fixed-overlay test localized CypA relative to the original DNA footprint. Repositioning the duplex supplied an explicit example in which it fits the actual near-start B assembly with both probe ends seated. In the original covering start, matched removal of CypA exposed fits in a subset of frames. The comparison addresses the added steric effect of CypA at a fixed geometry. A physical system could rearrange DNA, protein or probe upon association or removal, and a finite rigid panel can miss a fit.

What remains uncertain is which of these arrangements, if any, the molecule populates in solution. Preassembling the complex and choosing its orientation sets the initial condition. The branches did not exchange between the six reference neighborhoods. Their relative numbers were set by the design of the simulation. Pooling all observations would therefore give a fraction determined in part by how many covering starts we chose to simulate. The DNA integration also conditions on a fixed complex, and its steric overlap represents the volume required by a reference duplex. Protein flexibility, DNA deformation, chromatin, full-length TBXT and intermolecular binding equilibria can alter the outcome. The independent docking and affinity controls further constrain the interpretation of positive and negative predictions.

The immediate experimental question is whether intact B binds each protein, forms a ternary complex, and weakens single-T-box DNA binding specifically in the presence of CypA. Together with an independent measurement of ternary binding, a DNA-binding titration with matched vehicle, probe-only, CypA-only and probe-plus-CypA conditions would distinguish recruitment accompanied by competition from recruitment that leaves DNA binding intact. A matched probe impaired in CypA binding would help attribute an effect to recruitment. No physical probe or assay data were available for this study. Accordingly, the present result is a structural prediction that separates those experimental outcomes. It establishes neither a TBXT inhibitor nor a therapeutic effect in chordoma.
